Cortina d'Ampezzo city overview

Cortina d'Ampezzo sits at 1,211 metres in a bowl of Dolomite walls that UNESCO lists as World Heritage, and the town has hosted the Olympics twice. Its own ski area counts 33 lifts and 101 slopes, and the Dolomiti Superski pass it belongs to opens 1,200 kilometres of slopes and 450 lifts across twelve linked areas.

Longest run
Faloria Normale, 3,155 mOur pick among the Cortina lift companies' own published run lengths. Faloria Cristallo lists all 14 of its pistes with lengths, the next longest being Rio Gere at 2,556 m; on the Tofana side only Forcella Rossa carries a length, 2 km. Neither calls any run the area's longest. Cortina skiers also reach the longer Armentarola descent from the Lagazuoi, but that one ends in Alta Badia.
Day pass
Priced by date in three bandsDolomiti Superski prices the day pass by date - early and late-season promo, season, and high season - and publishes the rate only through its own price calculator, with 5% off online two days ahead. Its 2026/27 adult season pass is EUR 990 in advance sale to 24 December and EUR 1,060 after.

Stay overview

The town is spread along the valley floor under the Tofane, with the cable cars leaving from its edges. Corso Italia is the pedestrian spine; the outlying hamlets are quieter and cheaper.
