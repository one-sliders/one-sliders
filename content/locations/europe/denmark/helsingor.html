Helsingør city overview

Helsingor is a compact Øresund town where ferries, maritime history and Shakespearean castle walls meet on Denmark's north coast. Start at Kronborg Castle, step into the M/S Maritime Museum, then wander Helsingor old town before a ferry view across to Sweden. Most international arrivals use Copenhagen Airport and continue north by train.

City history

1200Around 1200, the first church, Saint Olaf's Church, was built.
1231The city was first mentioned as Hælsingør and the people as Helsinger in King Valdemar the Victorious's Liber Census Daniæ from 1231 (not to be confused with the Helsings of Hälsingland in Sweden).
1429He established the Sound Dues in 1429, which were a toll on the use of the Øresund.
1479The city's growing wealth further fuelled the growth of maritime transport through the Øresund Strait - from 1479 to 1581 the number of ships passing through the strait increased 6.5 times.
1672In 1672, Helsingør had grown to be the third-largest town in Denmark.
1943Adolf Hitler had ordered that all Danish Jews were to be arrested and deported to the concentration camps on Rosh HaShanah, the Jewish New Year, which fell on 2 October 1943.
13th centuryThe oldest parts of the cathedral of Helsingør date back to the 13th century and show that the fishing village, as Helsingør was then, had grown to become a town of importance.
1420sThe city as it is known today was founded in the 1420s by Danish King Eric of Pomerania.
1570sKrogen was rebuilt and expanded in the 1570s by king Frederik II and renamed Kronborg.
19th centuryUp until the middle of the 19th century, Helsingør was among the largest cities in the country.
TodayPlace names show that the Helsinger may have had their main fort at Helsingborg and a fortified landing place at Helsingør, to control the ferry route across the strait.
Regional roleWith that income, the king built a castle named Krogen or Ørekrog on the extreme northeastern tip of the island of Zealand.
Regional roleThe oldest known fortified building of Helsingør is Flynderborg, an early medieval fortress on a hill just south of the medieval city.
Regional roleDuring World War II, Helsingør was among the most important transport points for the rescue of Denmark's Jewish population during the Holocaust.

Stay overview

Stay in Helsingor old town for the easiest walks, the harbour area for castle and museum access, Marienlyst for spa and sea views, or Snekkersten for a quieter coastal rail base.
