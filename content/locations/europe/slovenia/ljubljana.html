Ljubljana city overview

Ljubljana is a Slovenia base best planned around named sights rather than a generic stopover. Start with Ljubljana Castle, add Triple Bridge, and use Prešeren Square to understand the city's history, street life and visitor rhythm. Ljubljana International Airport handles the main arrival flow, so hotel choice should balance the centre, transfer time and day-trip plans.

City history

Timeline

40021 people died due to the earthquake and some 10% of the city's 1,400 buildings were destroyed.
452In 452, it was destroyed by the Huns under Attila's orders, and later by the Ostrogoths and the Lombards.
1256In 1256, when the Carinthian duke Ulrich III of Spanheim became lord of Carniola, the provincial capital was moved from Kamnik to Ljubljana.
1279It was administered by the Counts of Gorizia from 1279 until 1335, when it became the capital town of Carniola.
1511After the 1511 Idrija earthquake, the city was rebuilt in the Renaissance style and a new wall was built around it.
1929In 1929, Ljubljana became the capital of the Drava Banovina, a Yugoslav province.
1941In 1941, during World War II, Fascist Italy occupied the city, and then on 3 May 1941 made Lubiana the capital of Italy's Province of Ljubljana with former Yugoslav general Leon Rupnik as mayor.
1991It retained this status until Slovene independence in 1991. Contemporary situation Ljubljana is the capital of independent Slovenia, which joined the European Union in 2004.
2011Their archaeological remains, nowadays in the Municipality of Ig, have been designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site since June 2011, in the common nomination of six Alpine states.

Notable eras

6th centuryIn the 6th century, the ancestors of the Slovenes moved in.
12th centuryUrban settlement started in the second half of the 12th century.
17th centuryIn the middle and the second half of the 17th century, foreign architects built and renovated monasteries, churches, and palaces and introduced Baroque architecture.

Regional role

During the subsequent reconstruction, some districts were rebuilt in the Vienna Secession style.

After World War II, Ljubljana became the capital of the Socialist Republic of Slovenia, part of the Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia.
