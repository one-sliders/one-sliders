Budapest city overview

Budapest is a Danube capital of thermal baths, grand cafes, ruin bars and hilltop views. Start at Buda Castle, cross the Chain Bridge area, then plan Parliament, the Jewish Quarter and Széchenyi Thermal Bath across different parts of the city. Budapest Airport is southeast of town, with bus and taxi transfers into the centre.

Stay overview

District V is the easiest first base, the Jewish Quarter is best for nightlife, Buda Castle District is quieter and scenic, and Terézváros balances culture, transport and hotel choice.

Hotels & areas

Budapest is split by the Danube. Pest is usually better for restaurants and nightlife, while Buda suits views and calmer historic stays if you accept more climbing or transit.

City history

106The Roman settlement – Aquincum – became the main city of Pannonia Inferior in 106 AD.
1361In 1361 it became the capital of Hungary.
1367After the foundation of the first Hungarian university in Pécs in 1367 (University of Pécs), the second one was established in Óbuda in 1395 (University of Óbuda).
1718In the 1699 Treaty of Karlowitz, these territorial changes were officially recognized as the end of the rule of the Turks, and in 1718 the entire Kingdom of Hungary was removed from Ottoman rule.
1848The national insurrection against the Habsburgs began in the Hungarian capital in 1848 and was defeated one and a half years later, with the help of the Russian Empire.
1851Between 1851 and 1910 the proportion of Hungarians increased from 35.6% to 85.9%, Hungarian became the dominant language, and German was crowded out.
1944In 1944, a year before the end of World War II, Budapest was partly destroyed by British and American air raids (first attack 4 April 1944).
1964Work on Erzsébet Bridge, the last to be rebuilt, was finished in 1964.
1970sIn the early 1970s, Budapest Metro's east–west M2 line was first opened, followed by the M3 line in 1976.
2002Andrássy Avenue (including the Millennium Underground Railway, Hősök tere, and Városliget) was added to the UNESCO list in 2002.
11th centuryBuda was probably the name of the first constable of the fortress built on the Castle Hill in the 11th century or a derivative of Bod or Bud, a personal name of Turkic origin.
19th centuryBudapest also became an important center for the Aromanian diaspora during the 19th century.
1950sThe new Communist government considered the buildings like the Buda Castle symbols of the former regime, and during the 1950s the palace was gutted and all the interiors were destroyed (also see Stalin era).
Regional roleIt was this compromise which opened the second great phase of development in the history of Budapest, lasting until World War I.
