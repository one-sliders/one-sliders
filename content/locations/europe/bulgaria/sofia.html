Sofia city overview

Sofia is a layered Balkan capital where Roman ruins, Orthodox churches and mountain views sit inside a working city. Start at Alexander Nevsky Cathedral, follow the Serdica ruins through the centre, then plan a half-day to Boyana Church or Vitosha Mountain. Sofia Airport is metro-linked, making arrivals unusually easy.

City history

117It became a municipium during the reign of Emperor Trajan (98–117).
339In 339 BC Philip II of Macedon destroyed and ravaged the town for the first time.
6th centuryIn 343 AD, the Council of Sardica was held in the city, in a church located where the current 6th century Church of Saint Sophia was later built.
447The city was destroyed in the 447 invasion of the Huns and laid in ruins for a century.
809Serdica became part of the First Bulgarian Empire during the reign of Khan Krum in 809, after a long siege.
1194It was once again incorporated into the restored Bulgarian Empire in 1194 at the time of Emperor Ivan Asen I and became a major administrative and cultural centre.
1530In 1530 Sofia became the capital of the Ottoman province (beylerbeylik) of Rumelia for about three centuries.
18th centurySince the 18th century the beylerbeys of Rumelia often stayed in Bitola, which became the official capital of the province in 1826.
3rd centuryIn the 3rd century, it became the capital of Dacia Aureliana, and when Emperor Diocletian divided the province of Dacia Aureliana into Dacia Ripensis (at the banks of the Danube) and Dacia Mediterranea, Serdica.
Modern cityAnother neolithic settlement was founded in the 3rd–4th millennium BC near the site of the modern National Art Gallery, which has been the traditional centre of the city ever since.
Regional roleIt gradually became the most important Roman city of the region.
Regional roleIt grew into an important fortress and administrative centre under Krum's successor Khan Omurtag, who made it a centre of Sredets province (Sredetski komitat, Средецки комитат).
Regional roleIt became the capital of the beylerbeylik of Rumelia (Rumelia Eyalet), the province that administered the Ottoman lands in Europe (the Balkans), one of the two together with the beylerbeylik of Anatolia.

Stay overview

Use the City Centre for the easiest first Sofia stay, Oborishte for quieter culture-focused hotels, Lozenets for business and park access, or Studentski Grad for lower prices and nightlife outside the core.

Hotels & areas

Sofia is practical by metro, so central hotels are not the only option. Still, first-time visitors usually do best near Serdica, Vitosha Boulevard or Oborishte for walkable food and sights.

Travel tips

Best base

Stay near Serdica or Vitosha Boulevard if you want restaurants, Roman ruins and metro lines close together.

Airport metro

The airport metro is straightforward, but taxis are useful for late arrivals or hotels away from stations.

Mountain weather

Vitosha can be much cooler than central Sofia, so take a layer even on warm city days.

Day trip

Rila Monastery is the classic long day trip, while Boyana Church is easier for a shorter outing.
