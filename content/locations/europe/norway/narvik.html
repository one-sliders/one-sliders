Narvik city overview

Narvik is a ski town at 68 degrees north where a gondola runs from the street into the mountain. Twenty-two runs drop up to 889 metres with the Ofotfjord below, the off-piste terrain falls 1,154, and the resort will host the 2029 Alpine World Ski Championships. The main season starts in February and spring skiing runs into May.

Stay overview

Narvik is a town first and a ski resort second, which is unusual this far north: the gondola leaves from the street, so a bed in town is a bed at the lift. Basecamp Narvik puts you on the slopes, and Camp 291's glass cabins trade walking distance for northern lights.

Travel tips

The gondola starts in town

Narvik's gondola runs straight from the town into the mountain, so no transfer is needed to reach the slopes.

From the first winter snowfall to spring skiing in May, with the main season starting in early February.

Groomed or not

Of the 22 runs, 17 are groomed and 5 are left ungroomed, 16 kilometres in total.

Narvik will host the 2029 Alpine World Ski Championships and is being built out for it.

1939In 1939, Germany's war industry depended upon iron ore mined in Kiruna and Malmberget in Sweden.
1940Winston Churchill realized that the control of Narvik meant stopping most German imports of iron ore during the winter of 1940.
