Trysil city overview

Trysil is a single cone of a mountain skied from four sides, and all four are on one SkiPass: Turistsenteret with the gondola and the adventure pool, Skihytta in the sun, Høyfjellsenteret high up, and Högegga where the steep slopes and the tree skiing are. Forty-one lifts serve 71 slopes over 685 metres of vertical.

What the mountain suits

Facts as the operator publishes them, not a score. Read the numbers and decide.

Families

  • Turistsenteret has 16 of its 23 slopes graded very easy or easy, plus a large children's area, an adventure pool and a spa.
  • Skihytta has a children's area and cabins with ski-in ski-out access.

Strong skiers

  • Högegga has 8 difficult slopes of 13 - more black runs than the other two charted areas together - with tree skiing and the resort's steepest terrain.
  • 685 metres of vertical.

Stay overview

The four areas ring one mountain, so the side you sleep on decides your first run rather than your whole week. Turistsenteret is the biggest and has the gondola; Högegga is the steep side; Skihytta and Høyfjellsenteret sit between them.

Travel tips

Which side

Turistsenteret has 16 of its 23 slopes graded very easy or easy; Högegga has 8 difficult of 13. They are opposite ends of the same mountain.

Two countries, one pass

A SkiPass of three days or more in Trysil includes two days in Sälen across the Swedish border.

The gondola

Trysil's gondola runs from Turistsenteret, the largest of the four areas.
