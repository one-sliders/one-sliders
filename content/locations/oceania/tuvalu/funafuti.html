Funafuti city overview

Funafuti works best as a base in Tuvalu when you anchor days around Funafuti Conservation Area, Funafuti Lagoon and Vaiaku. Use those sights to understand the city's history, street life and visitor rhythm. Funafuti International Airport handles the main arrival flow, so hotel choice should balance the centre, transfer time and day-trip plans.

City history

1819His vessel sailed under British colors, and in May 1819, de Peyster passed through the southern waters of Tuvalu and sighted Funafuti.
1841In 1841, the United States Exploring Expedition, led by Charles Wilkes, visited Funafuti.
1881Alfred Restieaux, a native of England, lived and worked as a trader on Funafuti from July 1881 until about 1888 or 1889.
1882In 1882, members of the US Fish Commission visited Funafuti on the USFC Albatross to investigate the formation of coral reefs on Pacific atolls.
1892In 1892, Captain Edward Davis of HMS Royalist provided a report describing the traders and trading activities he observed on each of the islands he visited.
1896Professor Edgeworth David of the University of Sydney participated in the 1896 expedition and led the 1897 effort.
1979The United States claimed Funafuti, along with based on the 1856 Guano Islands Act, and maintained this claim until 1983, when a treaty of friendship, concluded in 1979, went into effect.
1996In June 1996, the Funafuti Conservation Area was established along the western rim of the reef, encompassing six islets.
2050Tuvalu's vulnerability is underscored by projections indicating that by 2050, half of Funafuti could be submerged during high tides, with up to 95% potentially flooded by 2100.
1850sIn the 1850s, John (Jack) O'Brien became the first European to settle in Tuvalu.
