Apia city overview

Apia works best as a base in Samoa when you anchor days around Robert Louis Stevenson Museum, Palolo Deep Marine Reserve and Apia Market. Use those sights to understand the city's history, street life and visitor rhythm. Apia International Airport handles the main arrival flow, so hotel choice should balance the centre, transfer time and day-trip plans.

City history

1800Apia was originally a small village (the 1800 population was 304), from which the country's capital took its name.
1889The harbour was the site of a notorious 15 March 1889 naval standoff in which seven ships — from Germany, the US, and Britain — refused to leave the harbour, even though a typhoon.
1898Seumanutafa Pogai was high chief until his death in 1898.
1900Western Samoa was ruled by Germany as German Samoa from 1900 to 1914, with Apia as its capital.
1914In August 1914, the Occupation of German Samoa by an expeditionary force from New Zealand began.
1920New Zealand governed the islands, (as the Western Samoa Trust Territory) from 1920 until Samoan independence in 1962 – first under a League of Nations Class C Mandate and then, after 1945, as a.
1929On what became known as "Black Saturday" (28 December 1929), during a peaceful Mau gathering in the town, the New Zealand constabulary killed the paramount chief Tupua Tamasese Lealofi III.
1941During World War II the United States Navy built and operated Naval Base Upolu from 1941 to 1944.
1850sThe modern city of Apia was founded in the 1850s, and it has been the official capital of Samoa since 1959.
1900sThe country underwent a struggle for political independence in the early 1900s, organised under the aegis of the national Mau movement.
GrowthApia Village still exists within the larger modern capital of Apia, which has grown into a sprawling urban area that encompasses many villages.
