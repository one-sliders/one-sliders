Taipei city overview

Taipei works best as a base in Taiwan when you anchor days around Taipei 101, Chiang Kai-shek Memorial Hall and National Palace Museum. Use those sights to understand the city's history, street life and visitor rhythm. Taipei Taoyuan handles the main arrival flow, so hotel choice should balance the centre, transfer time and day-trip plans.

City history

1875In 1875, the northern part of the island was incorporated into the new Taipeh Prefecture.
1886In 1886, as work commenced to govern the island as a province, Taipeh was designated as the provincial capital.
1887It was formerly established as Taipeh-fu and was the temporary capital of the island in 1887 when it was declared a province (Fukien-Taiwan Province).
1895The romanized transcription of Taipeh was changed to Taihoku in 1895 when the Empire of Japan annexed Taiwan, based on the Japanese reading of the two characters.
1920During Japanese rule, Taihoku was incorporated in 1920 as part of Taihoku Prefecture.
1938The eastern village of Matsuyama (松山庄; modern-day Songshan District, Taipei) was annexed into Taihoku City in 1938.
1949After facing defeat from Communist forces, the ruling Kuomintang relocated the ROC government to Taiwan and declared Taipei the provisional capital of the ROC in December 1949.
1990In 1990, Taipei provided the backdrop for the Wild Lily student rallies that moved Taiwanese society from one-party rule to multi-party democracy by 1996.
OriginsPrior to the significant influx of Han Chinese colonists, the region of Taipei Basin was mainly inhabited by the Ketagalan, a Plains Indigenous people.

Regional roleTaiwan's Japanese rulers embarked on an extensive program of advanced urban planning that featured extensive railroad links.
Regional roleTaiwan's Kuomintang rulers regarded the city as the capital of Taiwan Province and their control as mandated by General Order No.
Regional roleHaving been established adjoining the flourishing townships of Bangka, Dalongdong, and Twatutia, the new prefectural capital was known as Chengnei (Chinese: 城內; pinyin: chéngnèi; Pe̍h-ōe-jī: siâⁿ-lāi), "the inner city", and government buildings were erected.
Regional roleAfter the Japanese takeover, Taipei, romanized into English as Taihoku following the Japanese language pronunciation, was retained as the capital.

National Palace Museum

National Palace Museum is a key Taipei culture stop, useful for understanding local history, collections and rainy-day planning near the main sightseeing route.

Longshan Temple

Longshan Temple anchors the historic and ceremonial side of Taipei, making it a practical stop for architecture, local memory and orientation.

Shilin Night Market gives Taipei a concrete street-life stop, with food, everyday shopping and local movement that helps visitors read the city.
