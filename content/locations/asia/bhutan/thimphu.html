Thimphu city overview

Thimphu works best as a base in Bhutan when you anchor days around Tashichho Dzong, Buddha Dordenma and Memorial Chorten. Use those sights to understand the city's history, street life and visitor rhythm. Thimphu Paro handles the main arrival flow, so hotel choice should balance the centre, transfer time and day-trip plans.

City history

1885In 1885, a battle was held at what is now the Changlimithang sports ground in Thimphu.
1952Reforms continued and in 1952 the decision was made to shift the capital from the ancient capital of Punakha to Thimphu.
1953The fourth king, who had established the National Assembly in 1953, devolved all executive powers to a council of ministers elected by the people in 1998.
1960Before 1960, Thimphu consisted of a group of hamlets scattered across the valley including Motithang, Changangkha, Changlimithang, Langchupakha, and Taba, some of which constitute districts of the city today (see below for district details).
1961In 1961, Thimphu officially became the capital of Bhutan.
1962Bhutan joined the Colombo Plan in 1962, the Universal Postal Union in 1969 and became a member of the United Nations in 1971.
1974The modern Changlimithang Stadium was built on the site in 1974.
2001The National Constitution Committee in Thimphu started drafting the Constitution of the Kingdom of Bhutan in 2001.
2005In 2005, the fourth king of Bhutan announced his decision to hand over the reins of his kingdom to his son Prince Jigme Khesar Namgyal Wangchuk.
2008In 2008, this paved way for the transition from absolute monarchic rule to a parliamentary democratic constitutional monarchy, with Thimphu as the headquarters of the new government, with the national defined objective of achieving.
Modern cityThe decisive victory opened the way for Ugyen Wangchuck, the first King of Bhutan, to virtually control the whole country.
TodaySince this time the sports ground has been of major importance to the city; football, cricket matches and archery competitions take place there.
Regional roleThe fourth king, Jigme Singye Wangchuck, opened the country for development and India provided the needed impetus in this process with financial and other forms of assistance.
Regional rolePunakha, the old capital of Bhutan, is on the windward side with broad-leaved trees dominating the topography.
