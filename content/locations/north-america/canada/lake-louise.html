Lake Louise city overview

Lake Louise sits at 1,538 metres in Banff National Park, and the ski resort above it spreads across three mountains and five separate faces - over 4,200 acres, 170 named runs and 991 metres of vertical, served by 13 lifts.

Stay overview

The hamlet sits by the Bow River with the ski resort across the valley and the lake itself four kilometres up the hill. Banff is 57 kilometres down the Trans-Canada Highway and has far more beds.

City history

1870sDuring the 1870s, construction of the Canadian Pacific Railway (CPR) began, with the route going through Bow Valley.
1882A Nakoda guide took CPR workman Tom Wilson to the site in 1882, and Wilson named it 'Emerald Lake'.
1892Lake Louise was added to Rocky Mountains Park in 1892, and has subsequently become an international tourist destination, made more accessible by its location near the Trans-Canada Highway.
1910A ca. 1890 CPR station was replaced in 1910, but continued to be used by the railway company for other purposes until it was donated to Heritage Park Historical Village, Calgary, in 1976.
1991The 1910 station was declared a heritage railway station by the federal government in 1991, having been well-preserved and operating today as a restaurant.

Source: Wikipedia: Lake Louise, Alberta
