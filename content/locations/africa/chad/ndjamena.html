NDjamena city overview

N'Djamena is Chad's capital, sitting where the Chari and Logone rivers meet opposite Cameroon across the water. First-time visitors should browse the Grand Marché for spices and crafts, see the National Museum of Chad for Sahelian history, and visit the Al-Mouna Cultural Centre for local art and events. N'Djamena International Airport lies just outside the centre, keeping arrivals close to most hotels.

Hotels & areas

N'Djamena hotel choice depends on how central you want to be: Centre-ville and Moursal put markets and daily life within reach, Sabangali adds river views, and Klemat offers a calmer, more residential alternative for longer visits.

Centre-ville

Centre-ville around Avenue Charles de Gaulle holds government buildings, banks and the Grand Marché, a practical base close to most main sights.

Travel tips

Best base

Choose Centre-ville for walking access to the Grand Marché and government district, or Sabangali for a livelier riverside stay.

Getting around

Arrange a driver through your hotel for getting around, since taxis are informal and fares should be agreed before departure.

Market visits

Visit the Grand Marché in the morning for the best selection of spices, textiles and crafts before the midday heat builds.

Documents and cash

Carry your passport and keep Central African CFA francs on hand, since card payments are uncommon outside a few larger hotels.

City history

1942On 26 January 1942, a lone German Heinkel He 111 of the Sonderkommando Blaich successfully bombed the airfield at Fort-Lamy, destroying oil supplies and ten aircraft.
1950Fort-Lamy received its first bank branch in 1950, when the Paris-based Banque de l'Afrique Occidentale opened a branch there.
1960sThe European district remained a separate part of the city into the 1960s, after independence.
1973Before its renaming in 1973, the city was known as Fort-Lamy, named after French army officer Amédée-François Lamy.
1979The city was partly destroyed during the Chadian Civil War in 1979 and again in 1980.
1980The city was occupied by Libya during the 1980–81 Libyan intervention as part of the Chadian–Libyan conflict, and the associated Transitional Government of National Unity.
1981Following differences between Goukouni and Muammar Gaddafi and international disapproval of Libyan intervention, the Libyan troops left the capital and Chad in 1981.
