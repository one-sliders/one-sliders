Dodoma city overview

Dodoma is Tanzania's official capital, a compact highland city built around the Bunge (National Assembly) and the country's fledgling wine industry. First-time visitors should view the Bunge building's public grounds, sample local vintages from the Dodoma Vineyards, and browse everyday goods at Uhindini Market. Dodoma Airport connects the city domestically, and Dodoma makes an interesting stop for travelers curious about Tanzania's planned-capital story.

Stay overview

Base in the city centre near Uhuru and Bunge for an easy first stay close to government offices and shops, or choose Area C for a quieter residential base. Kikuyu suits academic visitors near the university, while Uhindini works well for travelers focused on markets and shopping.

Travel tips

Best base

Choose the city centre near Uhuru and Bunge for an easy stay close to government offices, banks and restaurants.

Wine tasting

Set aside time for a Dodoma Vineyards tasting, since the region is Tanzania's main wine-producing area.

Airport transfers

Arrange a taxi from Dodoma Airport in advance, as the domestic terminal has limited ride-hailing availability.

Government access

Check current visitor rules before attempting to tour the Bunge grounds, since access can vary during parliamentary sessions.

City history

1932The site, the Dodoma region, had been considered a potential new capital as early as 1915 by the colonial power Germany; in 1932 by the British as a League of Nations mandate; and in the post-independence National Assembly in 1961 and 1966.
20th century20th century Originally a small market town known as Idodomya, modern Dodoma was founded in 1907 by German colonists during construction of the Tanzanian central railway.
1967In 1967, following independence, the government invited Canadian firm Project Planning Associates Ltd to draw up a master plan to help control and organise Dar es Salaam, which was undergoing rapid urbanisation and population growth.
1972The plan was cancelled in 1972, in part due to its failure to adequately address the city's historical and social problems.
1974In 1974, after a nationwide referendum, the Tanzanian government announced that the capital would move from Dar es Salaam to a more central location to create significant social and economic improvements for the central region and to centralise the capital within the country.
1996The official capital since 1996, Dodoma was envisaged as the first non-monumental capital city as opposed to the monumentality and hierarchy of other planned capital cities such as Abuja, Yamoussoukro, Brasília and Washington, D.C. It rejected geometrical forms such as grid iron and radial plans as inappropriate as the urban form was intended to undulate and curve with the existing topography and not in conflict with it so as to retain its rural ujamaa feel.
