Gaborone city overview

Gaborone is Botswana's low-rise, orderly capital, built up quickly after independence around Main Mall and the government enclave near the Notwane River. First-time visitors should explore the National Museum and Art Gallery for Tswana history and art, hike Kgale Hill for skyline views, and day-trip to the nearby Mokolodi Nature Reserve for rhino and giraffe sightings. Sir Seretse Khama International Airport connects Gaborone to the rest of southern Africa.

Notable eras

Civic roleGaberones, a shortening of "Gaborone's Village", was named after Chief Gaborone of the Tlokwa, whose home village (now called Tlokweng) was across the river from the Government Camp, the name of the colonial government.

Regional role

When Botswana gained its independence, Lobatse was the first choice as the nation's capital.

However, Lobatse was deemed too limited, and instead, a new capital city would be created next to Gaberones.

The city was chosen because of its proximity to a fresh water source, its proximity to the railway to Pretoria, its central location among the central tribes, and its lack of association with those.

Because the town was built so quickly, there was a massive influx of labourers who had built illegal settlements on the new city's southern industrial development zone.

This has led to many squatter settlements on undeveloped land.

Travel tips

Best base

Choose the CBD near Main Mall for a short business stay, or Broadhurst for good-value hotels within easy reach of the centre.

Getting around

Combined taxis and minibuses cover the city, but a rental car makes it easier to reach Phakalane, the airport and out-of-town reserves.

Weekday city

Gaborone is quiet at night and on weekends outside the malls, so plan dinners and errands around weekday business hours.

Day trips

Set aside half a day for Mokolodi Nature Reserve just south of the city, an easy wildlife outing without a long drive.
