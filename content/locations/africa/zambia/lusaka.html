Lusaka city overview

Lusaka is Zambia's plateau capital, a fast-growing commercial hub centred on the long, shop-lined Cairo Road. First-time visitors should browse crafts at Kabwata Cultural Village, learn Zambian history at the Lusaka National Museum, and pay respects at the Freedom Statue downtown. Kenneth Kaunda International Airport connects the city nationally and regionally, and Lusaka works well as a stopover before heading to Victoria Falls or South Luangwa's safari lodges.

City history

Timeline

452The section of line through Lusaka was built by the Mashonaland Railway Company, extending the line by 452 kilometres (281 mi) from Kalomo through to the mining town of Broken Hill (now Kabwe).
1905Lusaka was founded in 1905 as a water stop on the route and was named after a Chief Lusaaka, the leader of a nearby Lenje village.
1907The capital was initially at Kalomo, being switched to Livingstone in 1907.
1913By 1913, several stores and a hotel had opened, and they persuaded the BSAC to declare Lusaka as a recognised town and cede control of local affairs to them.
1931The water engineer's investigations concluded that there was sufficient groundwater, and the report confirming Lusaka as the planned capital was approved by the legislative council in July 1931.
1935Lusaka formally became the capital in May 1935, with a "Lusaka week" celebration scheduled to coincide with celebrations of George V's silver jubilee.

Stay overview

Base along Cairo Road for an easy first stay near shops, banks and the National Museum, or choose Kabulonga for quieter, upscale streets and dining. Woodlands and Rhodes Park suit business travelers wanting central offices and embassies within reach.

Travel tips
