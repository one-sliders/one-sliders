About the event

Coral spawning on the Great Barrier Reef is set by the moon, not the calendar: the Reef Authority puts it one to six nights after the October, November and December full moons, and which of the three applies depends on where the reef sits - inshore reefs go earlier, outer reefs in November and sometimes December. Whole colonies release their egg and sperm bundles at the same moment, at night, and the water fills with pink, yellow and orange clouds. Fertilisation depends on that synchrony, because the gametes of most species stay viable only a few hours. Dive operators plan around the full moon and announce trips a few weeks out; the Reef Authority fixes no date in advance, checked 27 November 2026.
