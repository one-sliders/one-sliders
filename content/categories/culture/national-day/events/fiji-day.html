Why this day

On 10 October 1970, Fiji gained independence from Britain, ending 96 years of British colonial rule. Independence was achieved peacefully through negotiation, and Fiji became the first Pacific island nation to hold the Olympic flag — a symbolic moment of national pride. The Queen remained head of state until the republican coups of 1987.

Fiji's colonial history began in 1874 when paramount chief Ratu Seru Epenisa Cakobau and other high chiefs signed the Deed of Cession, handing Fiji to Queen Victoria in exchange for protection from tribal warfare and foreign creditors. The British brought indentured Indian laborers to work the sugar cane fields — creating a complex multi-ethnic society that continues today.

Fiji Day is celebrated with a national parade in Suva, traditional ceremonies, meke dance performances and community gatherings. Schools stage historical pageants and the day ends with fireworks and musical events. The multi-ethnic character of modern Fiji — Indigenous Fijian, Indo-Fijian, Chinese and European communities — is reflected in the diversity of the celebrations.

What to eat
Kokoda

Raw fish marinated in citrus juice with coconut cream, tomato and chilli — Fiji's equivalent of ceviche.

Lovo

Earth oven feast — meats, fish, taro and vegetables wrapped in leaves and cooked in a pit fire.

Rourou

Taro leaf cooked in coconut cream — the most commonly served traditional Fijian vegetable dish.

Fish curry

Indo-Fijian spiced fish curry served with roti or rice — a staple from the Indian-influenced half of Fijian cuisine.

Cassava cake

Sweet baked pudding made from grated cassava, coconut cream and sugar — a festive Fijian dessert.

Duruka

Wild sugarcane flower — a seasonal Fijian delicacy cooked in coconut cream.

What to drink
Kava

Ceremonial drink made from ground pava root — mildly narcotic, earthy and central to Fijian social life.

Fiji Bitter

Fijian lager brewed locally — the most widely consumed beer on the island.

Fresh coconut water

Chilled green coconuts split open at roadside stalls — the essential Fijian refreshment.

Culture & traditions
Meke dance

Traditional Fijian dance and song — performed at ceremonies, receptions and Independence Day celebrations.

Kava ceremony

The ritual sharing of yaqona (kava) — essential at all formal gatherings, weddings and national events.

Coral reefs

Fiji's reefs are among the world's most diverse — diving and snorkelling are central to Fijian national identity.

Fire walking

The Beqa Island tradition of walking barefoot over white-hot stones — performed at ceremonies and festivals.
