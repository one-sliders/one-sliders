Why this day

Hungary celebrates St Stephen's Day on 20 August — the feast day of King Stephen I, the founder of the Christian Kingdom of Hungary who was canonised in 1083. Stephen was crowned on Christmas Day 1000 AD after receiving a crown from Pope Sylvester II, and he spent his reign Christianising Hungary, establishing its administrative structure and orienting it toward western Christendom.

The date of 20 August is traditionally associated with Stephen's canonisation and with the translation of his remains to Buda. The Holy Crown of Hungary and his right hand — the "Holy Right" — are national relics of the greatest significance. The Holy Right procession through Budapest on 20 August is one of the most important Catholic ceremonies in Hungary.

St Stephen's Day coincides with the foundation of the Hungarian state and is the country's most important national holiday — more significant than the other national holidays of 15 March and 23 October. The celebrations include a massive fireworks display over the Danube River, the Bridge Festival, the Holy Right procession and the Budapest Formula 1 Grand Prix usually falls nearby.

What to eat
Gulyás

Rich beef and paprika soup-stew — cooked in a cauldron over open fire, the most iconic dish of Hungary.

Lángos

Deep-fried dough topped with sour cream and grated cheese — the essential Hungarian street food.

Halászlé

Fisherman's hot paprika soup from the Danube and Tisza rivers — intensely spiced and flavourful.

Töltött káposzta

Cabbage rolls stuffed with pork and rice in a tomato-sauerkraut sauce — a Hungarian celebration staple.

Kürtőskalács

Chimney cake — dough wrapped around a spit, grilled until caramelised with sugar and walnuts.

Dobos torte

Layered sponge cake with chocolate buttercream and a caramel top — Budapest's most celebrated cake.

What to drink
Tokaji Aszú

Hungary's legendary sweet wine from the Tokaj region — described by Louis XIV as "the wine of kings".

Pálinka

Hungarian fruit brandy — apricot, plum and pear varieties are the most beloved traditional spirits.

Unicum

Bitter herbal digestif from the Zwack family — bitter, complex and drunk after a heavy Hungarian meal.

Culture & traditions
Budapest thermal baths

Budapest sits on over 120 thermal springs — Széchenyi, Gellért and Rudas baths are architectural wonders.

Danube fireworks

The 20 August fireworks display over the Danube is one of the largest in Europe — watched by over a million people.

Holy Right procession

The preserved right hand of Saint Stephen is carried through central Budapest in a solemn Catholic procession.

Liszt and Bartók music

Hungary has produced two of classical music's giants — performed at concert halls in Budapest and worldwide.
