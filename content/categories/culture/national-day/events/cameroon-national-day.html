Why this day

Cameroon celebrates National Day on 20 May, the anniversary of the 1972 referendum that replaced the federal system with a unitary republic. The date is different from the 1960 independence day of French Cameroon and reflects the country's bilingual and post-colonial complexity.

Cameroon had been divided between French and British administration after World War I. Independence, reunification and the later move to a unitary state all shaped the national story. That makes 20 May a state-unity holiday rather than a simple independence anniversary.

Celebrations include parades, school marches, military displays, speeches and cultural performances, especially in Yaounde. The day presents Cameroon as Africa in miniature, highlighting its regions, languages, football pride, music and food traditions.

What to eat
Ndole

Bitterleaf stew with peanuts, shrimp or meat, often described as a national dish.

Poulet DG

Chicken cooked with plantains and vegetables, a festive urban restaurant favourite.

Achu soup

Yellow palm-oil soup served with pounded cocoyam, associated with the Grassfields.

Eru

Leafy stew with waterleaf, palm oil and meat or fish from the southwest.

Koki beans

Steamed black-eyed pea pudding wrapped in leaves and coloured with palm oil.

Grilled fish

Whole fish with pepper sauce and plantains, especially popular in Douala.

What to drink
33 Export

Popular Cameroonian lager commonly served at bars and public celebrations.

Palm wine

Fresh palm sap drink with social and ceremonial roles in many regions.

Folere

Hibiscus drink served cold and sweet, especially in northern Cameroon.

Culture & traditions
Yaounde parade

The capital hosts the main National Day parade with schools, troops and officials.

Makossa

Urban dance music from Douala that became one of Cameroon's signature sounds.

Bamileke masks

Grassfields royal arts and masquerades are among Cameroon's strongest visual traditions.

Football culture

The Indomitable Lions give national celebrations a shared sporting language.
