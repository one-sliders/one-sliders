Why this day

The Feast of the Chair of Saint Peter on 22 February is one of Vatican City's most significant celebrations. It commemorates the authority of the Pope as the successor of Saint Peter — the apostle whom Jesus appointed as the foundation of the Church. The "chair" (cathedra) symbolises the teaching authority of the Bishop of Rome.

The celebration has ancient roots, predating Christianity itself — the Romans held ancestor feasts in late February, and the early Church adopted the date to honour Saint Peter's authority. By the 4th century the feast was well established in Rome, and it has been observed continuously since. The relic of Saint Peter's chair is kept in the apse of St Peter's Basilica in an elaborate golden monument by Bernini.

As Vatican City is the world's smallest sovereign state — 44 hectares within Rome — its national celebrations are inherently religious and ceremonial. The Pope presides over a solemn Mass in St Peter's Basilica, attended by cardinals, bishops and thousands of pilgrims from around the world.

What to eat
Cacio e pepe

Rome's most famous pasta — spaghetti with aged Pecorino Romano cheese and black pepper.

Supplì

Roman fried rice croquettes filled with mozzarella and tomato sauce — a street food staple.

Saltimbocca

Veal escalope with prosciutto and sage, pan-fried in white wine and butter.

Pizza bianca

Roman flatbread — olive oil, salt and rosemary, eaten plain or filled with mortadella.

Artichokes alla Giudia

Deep-fried whole artichokes opened like a flower — a Roman Jewish quarter classic.

Tiramisu

Rome's adopted dessert — mascarpone cream with espresso-soaked savoiardi biscuits.

What to drink
Frascati wine

White wine from the Castelli Romani hills just outside Rome — the traditional wine of the papal table.

Limoncello

Italian lemon liqueur served ice-cold at the end of a meal — popular across central Italy.

Espresso

Roman-style espresso — short, intense and taken standing at a bar.

Culture & traditions
St Peter's Basilica

The world's largest church — a pilgrimage destination for millions, designed by Michelangelo and Bernini.

Sistine Chapel

Michelangelo's ceiling frescoes and The Last Judgment — among the greatest artworks in human history.

Papal audience

Every Wednesday the Pope addresses pilgrims in St Peter's Square — a unique experience for visitors.

Vatican Museums

One of the world's great museum collections — 54 galleries of Greco-Roman sculpture, Renaissance painting and Egyptian antiquities.
