About The Open Championship

The Open Championship - golf's original major, first played in 1860 - is the oldest championship in the sport and the only major held outside the United States. Contested on a rota of seaside links courses in Britain and Ireland, it is defined by wind, rain, pot bunkers and the famous Claret Jug. Harry Vardon's six titles are the record.

Next editionThe Open Championship

The next Open Championship is played on one of the sport's historic links courses, this edition returning to the Old Course at St Andrews. The winner lifts the Claret Jug as 'Champion Golfer of the Year'.

FormatFour rounds of stroke play

The field plays 72 holes; after two rounds, the leading players and ties continue through the weekend.

HistoryGolf’s original championship

First played at Prestwick in 1860, The Open rotates across links courses in the United Kingdom and remains one of men’s four major championships.

RecordsThe Claret Jug

The champion receives the Claret Jug. Harry Vardon holds the record with six Open titles.
